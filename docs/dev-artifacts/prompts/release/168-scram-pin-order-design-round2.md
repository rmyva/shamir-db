### 2. P1, CONFIRMED: the TypeScript step misses the function that sends the proof
`runHandshake` in `crates/client/shamir-client-ts/src/core/protocol/protocol.ts` (about lines 144-183) decodes the challenge, derives the proof and SENDS it; `openSession` in `session/open.ts` (about 199-227) verifies the identity only after `runHandshake` returns. ### 3. P1, CONFIRMED: planned identity rotation becomes unreachable for a client holding the previous pin
Today a client with the previous pin completes the proof, then handles `ServerIdentityChanged` together with `auth_ok.rotation_in_progress` through `verify_rotation_recovery` and `ClientError::IdentityRotationPending` (`crates/client/shamir-client/src/client/handshake/auth_ok.rs` about 67-91). An early, fatal pin mismatch in the challenge phase prevents that path from ever running, yet section 272-292 says rotation stays intact. Decide and specify one policy with its consequences: (a) during a rotation window the challenge carries a chain (the previous identity key signs the new key and the transition deadline), so a client holding the previous pin can authenticate the server BEFORE the proof and still surface `IdentityRotationPending`; or (b) rotation requires out-of-band re-pinning and the document says that the in-band recovery path is removed, with what an operator has to do. Look at what the server side of rotation already signs (`rotation_in_progress`, `verify_rotation_recovery`) and prefer reuse. If the choice is a product decision, write it as an owner question with your recommendation, and design the recommended option completely.

### 4. P1, CONFIRMED by the reviewer: the guarantee is too broad on transports without a TLS exporter
On IPC, plain TCP and the browser-profile WSS the signed transcript uses zeros in the exporter field (your sections 102-115, 251-263). Nonces give freshness, but without channel binding they do not prove that the immediate peer IS the signer. Rewrite the guarantee (sections 208-218 and 297-303) so that it separates: (i) authentication of the endpoint that holds the pinned key (what the signature proves), (ii) resistance to a live relay that forwards the real server's signed challenge (holds only where the exporter binds the transport), and (iii) the remaining transport trust assumptions per transport, stated in one table: transport, exporter present or zero, what a rogue or relaying peer can still obtain. Do not claim more than the table supports.
